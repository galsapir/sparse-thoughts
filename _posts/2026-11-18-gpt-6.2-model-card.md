So I swear it’s not all going to be model announcements from now on, but it’s (again) this time. I think having all these announcements packed together actually makes it easier to see what’s similar and what’s different, and I’m curious to see if OpenAI still manages to put out the best ones.

It is the most impressive model announcement / card (like OpenAI knows how to create those) - it’s very readable and comprehensive in terms of benchmark results. The words are words, but the results of the Agents’ Last Exam are interesting, with 6.2 at max effort matching Gemini 4 Carbon on both performance and cost. It seems like OpenAI has made a lot of progress on model efficiency.[^effort] On the scientific workflow benchmarks (TBscience and Vals Science), the results look impressive compared with Fable’s, but Gemini 4 is missing from the charts. In the full system card, the health section has three benchmarks, with minuscule improvements, if any.

I think the focus here on everyday work goes head-to-head with Anthropic’s recent office suite announcements, and it’s probably in line with what many of the users are asking ChatGPT to do. Then they go eccentric (with the lamp builder excursion).

I think overall the way frontier labs highlight the way other companies utilize their models is very interesting. There are many, many testimonials like this in the announcement, across fields. It lets the domain experts answer ‘How much more useful is it than previous generations?’ I find that more convincing than internal benchmarks.

I think the part about the systems they use to prevent malicious model development is also worth mentioning. After the earlier incidents, it seems like OpenAI wants to share a bit more about what they’re doing to keep them from happening again. Perhaps the most interesting section worth mentioning from the full system card is this one about an [evaluation of unintended agent-to-agent communication](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol/unintended-agent-to-agent-communication) that they developed after the Hugging Face incident. They tried to recreate something like the message board the agents used in that incident. Supposedly, newer models are less likely to respond to messages from other models. We’ll see.

[^effort]: There’s some quirkiness here, as always. On this benchmark, `xhigh` gets better results at a lower cost.
